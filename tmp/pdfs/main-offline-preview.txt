ActDelta: Decision Relevance Is Learnable Offline but Does Not Yet Beat Periodic Sampling Online
                     A Measurement Study of Predictive Uplink Triggering for Edge-Offloaded Robot Policies
                                                               Anonymous Author(s)


     Edge-offloaded robot policies turn camera uploads into a recurring uplink cost. Predictive transmission usually suppresses an
     upload when the receiver can reconstruct the frame accurately, but a policy consumes observations to choose actions rather than to
     reproduce pixels. We test whether transmission should instead follow decision relevance: the action change induced by replacing
     a true observation with its prediction. Across four LIBERO suites, four fidelity metrics show weak and unstable association with
     action divergence (pooled Spearman −0.117 to −0.028), whereas a lightweight head over frozen world-model latents predicts
     divergence at Spearman 0.684 and AUROC 0.833. This offline separation does not yield an online win. In a frozen, matched-
     budget blind test, the learned trigger differs from periodic sampling by +3.13 pp at a 25% budget and −3.75 pp at 50%, with
     both confidence intervals including zero; an oracle using true runtime divergence also fails to outperform periodic sampling, and a
     content-blind Bernoulli scheduler at the same mean rate is not separated from the learned trigger anywhere on the budget sweep,
     while both separate cleanly from a fidelity-thresholded arm. Two diagnostics locate the failure: threshold triggering inflates the
     observation-age tail at a matched mean rate, and instantaneous action divergence separates episode outcomes far more weakly than
     observation age does. The result exposes an offline-to-online gap: ranking instantaneous action changes is learnable, but is not
     sufficient for closed-loop transmission scheduling.
